Notes:

- JRPG towns: starters usually contain standard buildings plus "training" buildings that teach the game to new players. Interestingly these towns are usually revisited as part of the main quest. Midgame towns usually are more complex versions than typical, and sometimes subvert conventions (Ioka Village doesn't trade in GP, Vector's normal inn is dangerous). Endgame towns are often very simple and rarely have standard shops, instead have special shops and lots of side-quest related buildings/NPCs
- FFVI: game is highly non-linear, with two worlds (WoB, WoR) and side branches in the early game. Figaro Castle, despite being a starter town, contains quest/dungeon rooms that are accessible later in the game. WoR mostly opens up new dungeons and variations of existing towns so there is no clear endgame town. Vector is also interesting as a midgame town as it subverts conventions.
- CT: game is highly non-linear and revisits locations often with changes. Towns actually get less functional (fewer functional buildings) and merchants get fewer as the game shifts to focusing on getting better gear via special quests and dungeons. A theme is fall of civilisations so many towns don't have standard merchants, e.g. Ioka merchant takes local currency (bones, leaves) instead of GP
- SF2: game is mostly linear but does revisit several towns. Normal buildings (shops) get less important later in the game as it's replaced by special endgame shops or hidden/quest items. Due to being an SRPG it has very few sidequests, and doesn't have an inn/tavern since characters auto-heal, instead has a church which revives dead characters for a fee
- Pokemon: game is an unusual JRPG in that instead of characters and equipment, players catch pokemon and learn/teach moves, an imperfect analogy. Likewise, instead of castles progressing the plot, the player collects badges from gyms in each town. Rare pokemon can be considered side quests. Although the game can be linear, in practice it is highly non linear as the player frequently revisits towns to collect more pokemon, and as HMs unlock areas, a metroidvania-like mechanic
- BG2: starts off in an extremely big and complex city with numerous merchants, none generic - sell multiple item types, sell special items. Game is quest-dense and doesn't follow typical town-wilderness-dungeon cycles, as most gameplay is quest-driven. Most buildings are quest-related in some way
- Oblivion: cities are quest-dense and starts/ends in a huge city, with multiples of each type of building. Quests serve as a way to encourage exploration and travel to different locations, breaking up the usual town-wilderness-dungeon loop. Trainers are counted as quests, and quests that involve multiple cities/buildings are double counted, but this is done to illustrate the high interactivity of this sandbox-RPG

Hypotheses:

- Functional vs flavour buildings ratio
- Building variety per town (distinct building types)
- Quest density per town / building
- Standard vs nonstandard towns (e.g. weapon+armour shops, vs specialised shop)
